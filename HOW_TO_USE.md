# How to Use Padhle

A quick walkthrough — written for the student actually using the app, not the developer. If you're the developer, see `README.md` and `TODO.md` instead.

## 1. Getting started (one-time)

1. Open the app and sign in with Google or an email + password.
2. Pick every exam you're preparing for — you can pick more than one (e.g. Class 12 Boards *and* JEE Main together).
3. Set the date of each exam. Padhle uses this for your countdown and to pace Study Sprints.
4. Wait a few seconds — Padhle builds your subject and chapter list automatically. You'll land on **Today** with everything ready.

## 2. Your daily rhythm

**Every day, Padhle will ask you one thing first:** *"What are your top 3 goals today?"* Keep it small — even one clear goal is enough. This becomes your "Today's Goal" card.

Then the loop is:

1. **Look at Today.** It shows exactly three things to do next — not a wall of forty tasks. If it's empty, go to **Planner** and add something.
2. **Tap "Start a focus session"** (or go to the **Focus** tab). Tell it what you're studying — subject, chapter, what kind of work (lecture / practice / PYQ / revision) — and how long. Then just study. The screen goes quiet on purpose: no notifications, no clutter, just a countdown.
3. **When the timer ends**, log how many questions you did (if it was practice/PYQ/revision). That's it — you just planted a tree in your Study Forest and earned XP.
4. **Check off tasks** in the Planner as you finish them.
5. **At the end of the day**, glance at your stats on Today: focus time, tasks done, questions done.

## 3. Planning ahead (weekly)

Use the **Planner** to lay out your week in advance, the same way you'd write a to-do list on paper:

> Example — planning next week for Hindi:
> 1. Hindi — Chapter 2 (Learning)
> 2. Hindi — PYQ practice
> 3. Hindi — Revision

Each of these is its own task in the Planner: pick a category (Board / JEE / PYQ / Revision / School / Mock), a subject/chapter, how long you expect it to take, and a date. Switch to **Week** or **Month** view to see the shape of your week and spot empty days.

> **Note on PYQs:** Padhle doesn't have a built-in question bank — you solve PYQs from whatever book, app, or coaching material you already use. Padhle's job is to help you *plan* that PYQ session, *track* that you did it, and *learn* from what you got wrong (see Error Book below). It's a coach, not a question bank.

## 4. Studying with a Sprint (your smart board)

If you want a short, visible target instead of "finish the whole syllabus," start a **Study Sprint** (7, 14, or 30 days) from the Sprints tab. Set a target — e.g. 300 questions, 80 PYQs, 2 mock tests, 14 focus hours.

Each sprint has a **board** (like a real project board, simplified):

```text
To Do ──────▶ In Progress ──────▶ Done
add task       working on it      ticked off ✓
```

1. Open a sprint → add tasks at the top of the board.
2. **Drag** a card to the next column as you work — or use the small ◀/▶ buttons under it (great on touch screens).
3. The sprint shows a progress ring, a "day dots" timeline, and a pace check: 🚀 **Ahead of schedule** / 🎯 **On track** / ⏳ **Behind schedule**.
4. When everything's done, tap **Mark sprint complete 🎉** (worth XP). Finished sprints live safely in the **Archive** tab — never thrown away.
5. **The app then reviews your sprint for you.** On the sprint page you get a "Sprint review": every goal achieved vs missed (with bars), your best and slowest days, the subject mix you actually studied, **what went well / what to fix**, and 2–4 plain suggestions for the *next* sprint (e.g. "set a smaller daily question target", "protect your weak days with a 15-min backup session"). Even an abandoned sprint gets an honest review so you can restart lighter and smarter.

Flow: `New sprint → Add tasks on board → To Do → In Progress → Done → Sprint complete → Review what went well/wrong → Next sprint, apply the suggestions`

## 5. Tracking chapter mastery

Go to **Syllabus** to see every chapter for your exam(s), each moving through: **Not Started → Learning → Practicing → PYQs → Revised → Mastered.** Tap the button on a chapter row to move it to the next stage as you actually study it. Use the filters (All / Weak / Not started / Mastered) to quickly find what needs attention.

## 6. Logging mock tests and learning from mistakes

After a mock test:

1. Go to **Tests & PYQs** → "Log test" and enter your scores.
2. If you got something wrong for a silly reason, a formula slip, or a concept gap — go to **Error Book** → "Log mistake" and note *why* you got it wrong and when you want to review it.
3. Once you've logged a few mistakes, a banner appears: **"Start Mistakes Sprint."** One tap and Padhle groups your mistakes by chapter and builds you a focused 7-day revision plan automatically.

## 7. Watching your progress (and having a little fun with it)

- Your **Study Forest** 🌳 grows one tree per focus session — check it on Today or your full forest on **Profile**.
- Your **streak** 🔥 counts consecutive days you've done something. Don't stress about protecting it perfectly — missing a day doesn't erase your longest streak, and restarting the same day is normal.
- Your **level** goes up as you earn XP from tasks, focus sessions, mastered chapters, mock tests, and finished sprints. It's simple on purpose: 100 XP = 1 level, no hidden math.
- **Achievements** unlock automatically as you hit milestones — check them on your Profile.
- **Let Padhle talk to you.** The card at the top of Today is a live Coach: in the morning it nudges you to set your top 3, when your streak is at risk it says so, on Monday it brings your last week's numbers and sets the tone, close to an exam (≤14 days) it turns urgent, and in the evening it wraps up your actual numbers with a gentle push. Watch it — it reacts to what you actually did.
- **Run your best** (Reports). A card shows your all-time best week of focus and best single day of questions — and how this week compares. It's a number to beat, not just a number to read.

## 8. Taking formatted Notes

The **Notes** page is your study notebook: formulas, mistake explanations, quick ideas.

1. Notes → **New note**.
2. Type away, then style with the toolbar — bold, italic, underline, headings, bullet/numbered lists, different **sizes**, **colors**, and **highlights**.
3. Pick a note color so it's easy to find at a glance; **pin** your most important notes to the top.
4. Everything is saved under your account and synced.

Flow: `New note → Write → Format → Pick color → Save → Pin if important`

## 9. Reports & Analytics (seeing where your time goes)

**Reports** answers "how's this week going?" — a Focus Score ring (0–100) and one plain-language suggestion pointed at your weakest habit. Switch between Week / Month / 30 days.

**Analytics** answers "where did my time actually go?"

- Pick a period: **Day / Week / Month / Year / Custom** (custom lets you choose any from/to dates, e.g. a whole custom year).
- Animated donut charts split your time **by subject** and **by activity** (Lectures / Practice / PYQs / Revision).
- A daily trend bar chart shows your rhythm.
- A green/red chip compares the current period with the previous one.

```text
Pick period (Day/Week/Month/Year/Custom)
        │
        ▼
Time by subject ─┐
Time by activity ┼▶ what worked & what didn't
Daily trend ─────┘
```

**Exporting:** on Reports, **CSV** downloads the numbers; **Print / PDF** produces a clean table you can save or hand to your parents.

## 10. Sharing with parents or a mentor

From **Profile → Parents & mentors**, add their email and copy the share link. They sign in with their **own** Google account and land on a **My students** hub that lists every student who has shared with them — no need to juggle separate links. Tapping a student opens their read-only snapshot.

Flow: `Add email → Share link → They sign in → My students hub → Read-only snapshot`

The snapshot shows: a **weekly Focus Score** ring, this week's focus/questions/tasks/mocks, a **6-week focus trend**, your **exam countdown**, **last-30-days** totals, **syllabus progress**, and a **recent activity feed** (focus sessions, tasks done, mock-test scores, daily check-ins). It's read-only — parents never see your password, notes, or edits.

## 11. Handy extras

- **Forgot password?** On the login screen tap "Forgot password?" and we'll email you a reset link.
- **Daily reminder** — Profile lets you set a "remind me to study at 8 pm" browser notification.
- **Offline** — if the network drops, a warning bar appears and you can keep working; everything syncs when you're back.
- **Dark mode** — toggle it on Profile (and on the login screen).

## Tips

- Don't try to plan every single day months ahead. Plan a week at a time and adjust.
- If you didn't finish something, don't panic — just re-plan it for tomorrow in the Planner.
- Use the Error Book honestly. The more specific your "why I got it wrong," the more useful your Mistakes Sprint will be.
- The app is meant to take *less* of your time the more you use it, not more. If you're spending more time organizing than studying, you're doing it wrong — just start a focus session.
- New to the app? Open **How to use** (avatar menu → How to use) for a tap-through guide with flow charts.
